# Call for Participation
{:.sub-page-header}
&nbsp;
{:.sub-page-border}

ACM HCOMP is the premier venue for disseminating the latest research findings on human-AI complementarity and alignment. Our community studies and designs systems that combine the complementary strengths of human and artificial intelligence to achieve outcomes neither could achieve alone, in ways that are ethical, safe, and intentional. This research builds on a foundation established by the HCOMP community during its first decade as an AAAI conference series focused on human computation and crowdsourcing.

HCOMP 2026 will be held as a co-located event with ACM Collective Intelligence (CI) 2026 from September 27-30, 2026, at the Virginia Tech Institute for Advanced Computing near Washington, DC, USA.

# Call for Full Papers and Talks
{:.sub-page-header}
{:#cfp}
&nbsp;
{:.sub-page-border}

HCOMP focuses on the emerging science and practice of human-AI complementarity and alignment. As AI systems become increasingly capable, the field is expanding from studying how humans contribute to building these systems to also studying how humans and AI systems work together as complementary partners. This broader perspective situates complementarity and alignment across the full lifecycle of AI systems, from how systems are built and evaluated to how they are used and governed in practice.

## Important Dates

All times are **Anywhere on Earth (AoE)**
- **June 1, 2026**: Abstracts Due
- **June 8, 2026**: Papers Due
- **July 31, 2026**: Notifications
- **August 13, 2026**: Camera Ready Due
- **September 27-30, 2026**: HCOMP 2026 Conference

## Submission Options

The two primary submission formats—full papers and talks—are intended to accommodate the different norms and requirements across the diverse fields represented in the HCOMP community.

*   **Full Papers (max 6000 words):** Will be assigned to a Program Committee member who will recruit 3 external reviewers and write a meta-review. Accepted full papers will be published in the archival ACM HCOMP conference proceedings available via the ACM Digital Library and assigned individual DOIs. *Note: authors submitting to the full papers track are expected to collectively review at least 3 papers.*
*   **Talks (formerly “extended abstracts”) (max 1500 words):** Will receive short reviews by two members of the Program Committee focusing on relevance and enthusiasm. Accepted talks will be made available on the HCOMP website but will **not** be archived in the ACM Digital Library nor assigned individual DOIs. This option is ideal for researchers who wish to submit their work to a journal later.

## Topics of Interest

Example topics for HCOMP include, but are not limited to:

### Research on human-AI complementarity
*   Human-AI collaboration, coordination, and co-adaptation
*   Division of labor, delegation, and supervisory control
*   Complementarity versus redundancy in human-AI systems
*   Hybrid workflows that combine human and AI strengths
*   Human-AI decision-making and problem solving
*   Human-AI interaction in organizational and societal settings

### Research on human-centered alignment
*   Alignment in training and in use
*   Trust, reliance, and calibration
*   Scalable human oversight
*   Steering, monitoring, and control of AI systems
*   Detecting, communicating, and repairing misalignment
*   Governance, accountability, and safety in human-AI systems

### Research on human contributions to AI systems
*   Crowdsourcing and human computation
*   Human feedback, preference learning, and evaluation
*   Data collection, annotation, and quality assurance
*   Bias, fairness, and responsible data practices
*   Human roles in the development, assessment, and governance of AI systems

## Policies and Requirements

**Anonymity.**
HCOMP 2026 adopts a double-blind review process for both full papers and talks. Submissions must be fully anonymized by removing author names, affiliations, and identifying details.

**ACM Open Access.**
Starting January 1, 2026, ACM is transitioning to 100% Open Access. Full papers will be published under [ACM Open Access](https://www.acm.org/publications/openaccess). Authors affiliated with participating institutions will not require APCs. For others, a transition subsidy is available ($250 for members / $350 for non-members).

**Policy on LLMs.**
*   **For Reviewers:** LLMs are **NOT** allowed for writing reviews or meta-reviews. Sharing paper content with an LLM is strictly prohibited.
*   **For Authors:** Text generated from LLMs must be clearly marked where used for purposes beyond editing the author’s own text. Please review the [ACM Policy on Authorship](https://www.acm.org/publications/policies/new-acm-policy-on-authorship).

**Double Submission Policy.**
Full papers must represent original work and should not be under simultaneous peer-review elsewhere. Talk submissions **can** be based on previously-published work, provided they are clearly cited.

**Preprints.**
We do not prohibit posting preprints (e.g., on arXiv) but ask that authors refrain from publicizing the research on social media or with the press until the review process is complete to maintain double-blind integrity.

## Awards
HCOMP 2026 will recognize one best full paper, one best talk, and one best student work. We will also recognize outstanding reviewers.
